The Extraordinary Life and Legacy of Amou Haji, the Iranian Hermit Who Avoided Water for Decades

By WeirdHub Team • 7 November, 2026

A Solitary Existence in Dezgah and the Deep Belief That Kept Him Away From Water

In the remote village of Dezgah, situated in the Fars province of southern Iran, an extraordinary human story unfolded over more than six decades. Amou Haji, an elderly Iranian man, chose to live a life completely removed from the standard conventions of modern society and personal hygiene. For almost sixty-seven years, he famously avoided taking a bath, driven by a deeply entrenched personal belief that washing himself with water would inevitably lead to severe illness and physical decline. This unique conviction dictated every single aspect of his daily routine and gradually transformed him into one of the most intriguing solitary figures of the contemporary world. Fearing that water and cleanliness would strip away his immunity or bring about sickness, Amou Haji retreated to the quiet outskirts of the village, spending his days and nights completely exposed to the natural elements. During the early years of his solitary existence, he slept in a simple hole dug directly into the rough earth, seeking basic shelter within the ground itself. Seeing his tough living conditions, the compassionate local residents of Dezgah eventually came together to assist him, constructing a modest open cinderblock brick shack so he could have a rudimentary roof over his head. Despite his harsh surroundings and total lack of modern comfort, he remained steadfast in his choice of living, fully embracing his rugged lifestyle. Over time, word of his extreme habits spread far beyond his isolated Iranian village, eventually reaching global media outlets. International news agencies and journalists began covering his incredible story, bestowing upon him the moniker of the world's dirtiest man. Yet, behind this startling title was an individual who simply sought peace and lived according to his own unwavering beliefs, surviving out in the quiet desert landscape for decades without standard amenities.

The Unconventional Habits and Global Recognition of an International Curiosity

Amou Haji's unconventional choices extended far beyond his strict avoidance of soap and water, shaping a diet and lifestyle that continuously surprised observers around the globe. Rather than consuming conventional home-cooked meals, he regularly sustained himself by eating rotten meat, showing a particular preference for decaying porcupine meat that he found in his surroundings. When it came to smoking, his habits captured immense attention worldwide. Amou Haji was often documented smoking dried animal feces packed into a makeshift pipe, choosing this startling material over standard tobacco products. On other occasions, when gifted commercial cigarettes by curious visitors or sympathetic locals, he would light multiple cigarettes at the exact same time, puffing away on several cigarettes simultaneously in a striking visual display. Over the years, the kindly local community of Dezgah made several attempts to persuade him to clean himself, genuinely concerned for his personal well-being. On one memorable occasion, well-meaning locals even tried to take him to a nearby river for a bath, but Amou Haji jumped out of the moving vehicle to escape, refusing to let the water touch his skin. His astonishing life, filled with such bizarre yet verified habits, eventually caught the attention of filmmakers. In 2013, a short documentary film titled The Strange Life of Amou Haji was officially released, chronicling his everyday existence, his unique philosophy, and his solitary survival in the harsh Iranian environment. The film provided international audiences with an intimate look into the daily realities of a man who had completely turned his back on modern sanitation.

The Fateful Wash in 2022 and the Final Days of a Unique Figure

For nearly seven decades, Amou Haji remained firm in his resistance to water, maintaining his unwashed status well into extreme old age. However, in 2022, a major turning point occurred when the local residents of his village finally managed to persuade him to break his lifelong rule. After persistent pressure and gentle encouragement from the community, the elderly hermit yielded to their requests and allowed himself to be washed, marking the first time in over sixty-six years that his body was thoroughly cleaned. Tragically, just a few months after undergoing this long-awaited wash, Amou Haji fell ill. On October 23, 2022, he passed away at the advanced age of ninety-four in the village where he had spent most of his life. His death generated widespread headline news across the globe, with major international publications like BBC News, The Guardian, and CNN reporting on the passing of the famous hermit. While many observers naturally wondered whether the sudden change in his hygienic routine had any connection to his rapid health decline, medical authorities and official reports emphasized that there was no direct or verified link between his bath and his subsequent death. Given his advanced age of ninety-four, his passing was widely viewed as the natural end to a remarkably long and unique life. Today, the legacy of Amou Haji survives as one of the most incredible stories of human endurance and singular devotion to a lifestyle entirely separate from modern society, leaving behind a fascinating historical record that continues to intrigue people all across the globe.
