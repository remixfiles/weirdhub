She Went on Six Dates a Week to Avoid Buying Groceries, Saving Over 15000 Dollars in New York City

By George Alpha Ben • 26 September, 2026

The High Price of Living in Manhattan and an Unintentional Strategy

Living in New York City often forces young professionals into extreme financial acrobatics just to keep their heads above water. Back in 2016, Vivian Tu was working as a junior equities trader on Wall Street at JP Morgan, earning an impressive base salary of around 95,000 dollars a year. On paper, it looked like a dream corporate start, but the brutal reality of Manhattan living quickly stripped away that illusion. After paying steep rent in Hell's Kitchen, managing daily subway commutes, and handling high city taxes, her take-home pay evaporated faster than expected. Grocery shopping in her neighborhood was notoriously expensive, often forcing her to haul heavy grocery bags fifteen blocks across town on foot from Trader Joe's just to secure affordable food. During that same transitional period, being fresh to the metropolis and eager to build a social circle, she actively joined dating apps to meet new people and search for meaningful companionship. As dinner invitations piled up across the city's vibrant dining scene, an unexpected financial pattern emerged. Her dates almost always picked up the bill at various restaurants, which suddenly eliminated her need to stock up her own refrigerator. Over time, she calculated that avoiding the supermarket spared her wallet approximately 150 dollars every single week, quietly compounding into a massive 15,600 dollars in personal food savings over a two-year stretch.

The Internet Explosion and the Nuanced Reality Behind the Numbers

Years later, when Vivian decided to share this eccentric budgeting byproduct in a casual, tongue-in-cheek TikTok video, the internet instantly ignited. Millions of viewers across North America and Europe flooded the comment section, sparking fierce international debates over dating culture, modern survival tactics, and urban economics. Some applauded her as an ultra-clever budget hacker who turned romantic outings into a survival shield against corporate burnout, while others harshly criticized her approach, accusing her of exploiting dates for free gourmet meals. However, the viral sensation painted only half the canvas. In a comprehensive interview with Elite Daily, Vivian set the record straight and dismantled the sensationalist rumors. She candidly explained that the claim of going on six dates every single week was heavily exaggerated for humorous social media effect. In genuine day-to-day life, she typically went on one to three dates weekly, and she frequently offered to split the bill or pay her share. More crucially, she stressed that securing free food was never her premeditated mission or motivation. Entering the dating scene with calculating, dishonest intentions was something she firmly warned against, noting that misleading people simply to cut domestic expenses generates toxic relationship karma and emotional frustration on both sides.

From Hustling Wall Street Trader to a Globally Trusted Wealth Guide

What began as a chaotic, humorous chapter in a young professional's New York journey ultimately transformed into a launching pad for an entirely different mission. Vivian realized that millions of young people across the globe were silently struggling with basic personal finance, suffocating living expenses, and the awkward social pressures of modern dating. Inspired by these widespread economic frustrations, she stepped away from the trading floor to establish her viral educational persona, Your Rich BFF. Stripping away dry financial jargon, she committed herself to teaching accessible, transparent money management skills to people who were never taught how to invest, negotiate salaries, or budget effectively. Rather than endorsing reckless life hacks or depending on dinner dates to balance a bank account, she now advocates for disciplined investing, emergency funds, and structured budgeting. Her practical advice earned her a spot on the prestigious Forbes 30 Under 30 list and produced bestselling financial books that guide audiences worldwide toward true economic autonomy. The story that once shocked the internet now serves as an amusing reminder of a time when surviving the big city felt impossible, standing in stark contrast to the empowered financial independence she champions for millions of followers every single day.
