Pixar Secretly Flew a DVD to a Dying Child's Bedside, and What Happened Next Stunned the World

By WeirdHub Team • 5 September, 2026

A Race Against the Relentless March of Time

In the spring of 2009, movie theaters worldwide were buzzing with previews for Up, a vibrant Pixar adventure about chasing dreams across the skies. In Huntington Beach, California, ten-year-old Colby Curtin watched the trailers with sheer wonder. She had been bravely battling a rare, aggressive vascular cancer for three years, and watching this colorful animated journey became her ultimate wish. By late May, as the film debuted on the big screen, Colby’s health took a steep and tragic downturn. Her mother, Lisa, fought desperately to arrange a hospice wheelchair so her daughter could visit a local cinema, but delays left them stranded at home. By June 9, Colby was completely bedridden and drifting in and out of consciousness. Sensing that time was rapidly running out, a close family friend named Terrell Orum began calling every contact number he could find at Pixar and Disney, determined to make Colby's fading dream come true before it was too late.

When Hollywood Broke Every Rule for Love

Corporate Hollywood is known for strict security, digital rights, and red tape, yet Colby’s story shattered every protocol. Within hours of hearing the urgent plea, a Pixar employee boarded a flight to Southern California carrying an unreleased DVD copy of Up, accompanied by plush toys and a special adventure scrapbook. Arriving at the Curtin home on June 10, the visitor set up a private screening right by the little girl's bed. By then, Colby was too exhausted and weak to open her eyes, but she knew the moment had arrived. Leaning close over the pillow, Lisa began describing every frame in gentle whispers, narrating the floating balloons, the grumpy old adventurer, and the loyalty of the young explorer. Colby listened intently to the soundtrack and her mother’s voice, absorbing every heartbeat of the cinematic journey. As the final credits rolled, Lisa asked her daughter if she enjoyed the film, and Colby gave a soft, peaceful nod.

The Final Ascent Beyond the Clouds

Just seven hours after the private screening concluded, Colby peacefully passed away, surrounded by the family who had fought so hard to give her joy. For Lisa Curtin, the movie was no longer just an animated feature about balloons and far-off lands; it became a profound symbol of her daughter’s transition. Seeing the house lift off and drift upward into the heavens gave the grieving mother a comforting reminder of Colby's gentle spirit soaring free from pain and illness. When regional and national news outlets later verified the incredible act of kindness, readers across North America and Europe were deeply moved by how human empathy eclipsed blockbuster secrecy. Colby never saw the colorful images with her eyes, but in her final hours on earth, she felt the true magic of human compassion lifting her soul toward the sky.
