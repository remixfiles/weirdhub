The Cat Who Ran a Town for Twenty Years, and Nobody Ever Voted Him Out

By Reporter of WeirdHub • 18 September, 2026

A Small Alaskan Town With No Interest in Human Politics

In 1997, the residents of Talkeetna, Alaska, a quiet town of fewer than a thousand people near Denali National Park, found themselves unimpressed by the human candidates running for local office. Rather than settle for a choice they didn't believe in, the town did something almost nobody expects from local politics: they wrote in the name of a scruffy, tailless kitten instead. That kitten, later known simply as Stubbs, became the honorary mayor of Talkeetna, a title he would hold for the next two decades. Technically, Talkeetna is only a historic district and has no legal mayoral office, so the position was symbolic from the very beginning. But symbolism has power, and in this case it turned an ordinary orange cat into a local legend. Stubbs made his home at Nagley's General Store, greeting visitors, lounging near the register, and occasionally wandering into the West Rib Pub next door as if checking in on constituents. He had an odd but charming habit of drinking water, and sometimes catnip, out of a margarita glass, which only added to his larger-than-life reputation. Word of the small town's small mayor eventually spread far beyond Alaska, turning a local joke into an internationally recognized story.

From Local Joke to International Tourist Attraction

What began as a playful act of protest against uninspiring candidates slowly grew into something much bigger than anyone in Talkeetna could have predicted. As news outlets picked up the story over the years, Stubbs became a genuine tourist draw, pulling in dozens of visitors every single day who were traveling through the region, often on their way to Denali. Reports from his caretakers noted that more than seventy five percent of people who stopped at the store asked the same question: where is the mayor? Cards, letters, and gifts poured in from admirers around the world who had heard about the cat running a town with no human mayor at all. Life was not always easy for Stubbs, however. In 2013 he survived a frightening attack by a dog, an incident serious enough to require treatment at a veterinary hospital, and he also had to outlive a false rumor of his own death that circulated at one point. Through it all, he remained a fixture of the town, aging gracefully as the unofficial face of Talkeetna. Even skeptics could not resist the story completely; while one opinion writer for a regional newspaper insisted the entire tale was exaggerated or untrue, that single dissenting voice was drowned out by years of firsthand accounts, photographs, and national wire service coverage confirming that yes, this cat truly did serve as the town's honorary leader.

A Twenty Year Term That Finally Came to an End

By late 2016, age had begun to catch up with the aging mayor, and Stubbs spent more time resting at home rather than making his usual rounds through the store and bar. Then, in July of 2017, his family announced the news that so many fans had been dreading. Stubbs had gone to bed as usual one evening and simply did not wake up the next morning, passing peacefully at the age of twenty. In a heartfelt statement, his owners, the Spone family, described him as a trooper until his very last day, someone who spent his final hours seeking affection, wanting to be petted, and curling up close to the people and fellow cats who loved him. His death made headlines across major outlets, from the Associated Press to CBS News, a remarkable amount of attention for what was, on paper, just the passing of a house cat. Yet Stubbs was never just an ordinary house cat. He was proof that a small, forgotten town could capture the world's imagination through nothing more than a shared joke that refused to fade away. In the years since, his story has continued to circulate as one of those rare, feel good pieces of internet folklore that turns out, upon closer inspection, to be entirely true. Talkeetna never did get another human mayor. Instead, the town simply waited to see which whiskered candidate might one day be ready to take up the leash of office.
