The Drunken Moose Who Climbed Too High: Sweden's Strangest Night of 2011

By Robert Willium • 13 September, 2026

A Midnight Roar In A Quiet Swedish Garden

On a quiet evening in early September 2011, a man named Per Johansson came home from work in the small town of Saro, near Gothenburg, Sweden. As darkness settled over the neighborhood, he suddenly heard a loud, deep bellowing sound coming from the garden next door. At first, he thought someone was playing a prank, laughing and making noises just for fun. Curious, he walked over to check, and what he found left him completely stunned. A full grown wild moose, known in Europe as an elk, was tangled high up in the branches of an apple tree, with only one leg still touching the ground. The animal was struggling, clearly unable to free itself, and the noise Johansson had heard was its desperate cry for help. It turned out this was not the moose's first strange appearance that day. A neighbor recognized the same animal as one that had almost collided with her car earlier, and she suspected even then that something was off about its behavior. Nobody imagined the story would end with the poor creature stuck dramatically inside someone's backyard apple tree, swaying awkwardly among the branches as if it had lost all control of its enormous body.

Fermented Apples And A Very Bad Decision

As it turned out, the explanation behind this bizarre scene was both simple and oddly relatable. Every autumn in Sweden, apples fall to the ground and slowly begin to rot, fermenting naturally in the cool air and turning into a kind of accidental alcohol. Wild moose absolutely love eating these apples, and eating too many of the fermented ones can genuinely get them drunk, much like a person who has had one too many drinks at a party. Experts say this is a fairly common occurrence in Swedish gardens during harvest season, though usually the intoxicated animals simply stumble around or fall asleep somewhere safe. This particular moose, however, seemed determined to reach even more apples still hanging higher up in the tree. Reaching upward while already unsteady on its feet, it lost its balance completely and became hopelessly trapped among the branches, unable to climb down or free its massive legs. Local residents later joked that the animal had clearly been on what they called an all day bender, wandering around town in a confused, tipsy state long before anyone realized just how much trouble it was about to get itself into that night.

Rescue, Recovery, And A Walk Of Shame Into The Woods

Once Johansson realized the seriousness of the situation, he immediately called for help, and soon a hunter, local police, and the fire brigade all arrived at the scene together. Working carefully, the rescue team began sawing off some of the tree's branches to gently loosen the moose without causing it further injury. After a tense effort, the animal was finally freed and dropped to the ground, where it lay still for a while, seemingly unconscious from both the fall and the alcohol still in its system. Emergency responders checked on it and confirmed the moose was alive, though clearly exhausted, and Johansson was asked to keep watch overnight in case the animal needed further help. True to expectations, the moose spent the rest of the night quietly recovering on the lawn, sleeping off its unusual adventure under the open sky. By the next morning, it slowly got back onto its feet, looking groggy and worn out, before stumbling off into the nearby forest in what locals affectionately described as a walk of shame. The story quickly spread beyond Sweden, picked up by international news outlets and shared with amusement across the world, turning one ordinary autumn evening into an unforgettable tale about an apple loving moose who simply could not resist one more bite.
