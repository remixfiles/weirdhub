The Great Alcatraz Escape of 1962: How Three Men Vanished into History

By WeirdHub Team • 6 November, 2026

The Mastermind Plan and the Ingenious Tools of Escape

On the night of June 11, 1962, one of the most audacious and legendary prison breaks in modern history took place inside the walls of Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary, an isolated island fortress surrounded by the freezing waters of San Francisco Bay. The daring operation was orchestrated by three inmates named Frank Morris, John Anglin, and Clarence Anglin. For many months leading up to their escape, the trio meticulously devised a complex plan directly under the watch of prison authorities. Working in total secrecy inside their individual cells, they gathered mundane, everyday objects to forge their escape equipment. Their primary digging implements consisted of ordinary metal spoons, butter knives, and an improvised mechanical drill that they had cleverly crafted using the small electric motor from a discarded vacuum cleaner. With extraordinary patience and determination, the men worked during quiet hours, gradually chipping away at the moisture-damaged concrete and plaster surrounding the ventilation ducts in their cell walls. Once the wall openings were widened sufficiently, they accessed an unguarded utility corridor running behind their cells, which served as their covert workshop and pathway to the roof. To ensure they had enough time to flee without raising an immediate alarm, the trio created extraordinarily convincing dummy heads to place on their cots. These decoy heads were laboriously built from a mixture of bar soap, toilet paper, paint, and genuine human hair collected from the floor of the prison barbershop. When arranged carefully under blankets on the night of June 11, 1962, the realistic dummy heads successfully fooled the night guards during routine headcounts, providing the escapees with a crucial head start as they climbed up the ventilation shafts, accessed the roof, and made their way down toward the shore of the island.

Into the Chilly Waters of San Francisco Bay

After reaching the exterior perimeter of the island penitentiary undetected, Frank Morris and the Anglin brothers executed the next critical phase of their dangerous plan, which required surviving the perilous waters of San Francisco Bay. Over the course of their preparation, the men had managed to acquire and combine more than fifty stolen rubber raincoats to manufacture essential flotation equipment. Using these raincoats, they constructed a makeshift inflatable raft along with custom life jackets to keep themselves afloat in the open water. Under the cover of total darkness on the night of June 11, 1962, the three men launched their crude rubber craft into the cold, swirling currents of the bay and vanished into the night. When correctional officers finally discovered the fake dummy heads in the cells the following morning, a massive joint search operation was immediately launched across the region by law enforcement agencies. The Federal Bureau of Investigation opened an exhaustive, long-running investigation to trace the men and locate any physical remains or apparatus. After analyzing water conditions, following up on potential leads, and searching the surrounding waters for seventeen years, the FBI officially closed its active case file in 1979. Federal investigators concluded that while the inmates had successfully broken out of the prison facility, they most likely succumbed to hypothermia and drowned due to the extremely low temperatures and swift, unforgiving currents of San Francisco Bay. However, despite the FBI closing its active investigation, another federal law enforcement body took a different path. The United States Marshals Service chose to keep its official case open and active, maintaining an ongoing file that remains open to this day pending conclusive evidence.

The 2013 Letter and an Enduring American Mystery

Decades after the mysterious getaway took place, the unsolved case experienced a dramatic turn of events in 2013 when a surprising letter arrived at the San Francisco Police Department. The letter, which was written under the name of John Anglin, presented a startling claim regarding the fate of the three escapees. According to the document, Frank Morris, Clarence Anglin, and John Anglin had all successfully survived their treacherous transit across San Francisco Bay on that fateful night in June 1962. The letter asserted that the trio had safely reached land and lived out the remainder of their adult lives in secrecy. Upon receiving the unexpected correspondence, federal authorities subjected the letter to intensive forensic examination in an attempt to verify its origin. Forensic experts conducted detailed handwriting comparisons against historical writing samples from John Anglin and attempted to extract usable DNA evidence from the paper and envelope. However, the official laboratory testing yielded inconclusive results, meaning analysts could neither confirm nor rule out the authenticity of the letter or its sender. Because the validity of the 2013 letter could not be established with certainty, the final fate of Frank Morris and the Anglin brothers remains one of the great unresolved questions in modern criminal history. Today, the 1962 escape from Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary continues to captivate the public imagination, standing as one of America's most famous and enduring unsolved mysteries.
