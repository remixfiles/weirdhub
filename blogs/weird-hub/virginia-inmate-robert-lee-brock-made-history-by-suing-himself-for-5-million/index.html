The Prisoner Who Sued Himself for Five Million Dollars and Expected the State to Pay

By Nadia Hanna • 23 September, 2026

A Bizarre Plan Hatched Behind Cold Prison Bars

In 1995, deep inside the walls of the Indian Creek Correctional Center in Chesapeake, Virginia, an inmate was serving a heavy twenty-three-year sentence for breaking and entering alongside grand larceny. His name was Robert Lee Brock, and like many individuals enduring long decades behind steel doors, he spent countless quiet hours turning over the mistakes of his past. One summer night back in July 1993, Brock had heavily consumed alcohol, an act that directly shattered his personal religious beliefs and moral convictions. In his drunken state, he committed the crimes that eventually brought local police to his doorstep and placed him in handcuffs. Most people locked away in a cell might curse the arresting officers, blame an unforgiving judge, or regret their reckless decisions in private. But Brock was not an ordinary convict, and his mind worked along an unprecedented legal wavelength. He became obsessed with holding someone strictly accountable for violating his civil rights, derailing his life, and landing him behind bars. Looking into his tiny cell mirror, he decided that the true villain who had sabotaged his freedom was none other than Robert Lee Brock himself. With nothing to lose and years of time on his hands, he picked up a pen and started drafting one of the strangest legal documents in modern courtroom history.

A Handcrafted Five Million Dollar Masterpiece of Legal Acrobatics

Brock meticulously wrote a seven-page handwritten civil rights complaint naming himself as both the plaintiff and the primary defendant. In this extraordinary filing, he alleged that his drunken self had severely violated his own personal and religious rights, which directly caused his current incarceration. Because of this self-inflicted spiritual disaster, Brock formally demanded five million dollars in civil damages from himself. Of course, suing oneself usually presents a major financial roadblock, since taking money from your left pocket and putting it into your right pocket achieves nothing. However, Brock had engineered what he believed was a foolproof financial loophole. He argued that as an inmate without employment, property, or independent income, he had legally become an official ward of the state of Virginia. Therefore, he asserted that the state government was legally obligated to cover all his financial debts and legal liabilities while he remained incarcerated. Under his proposed settlement, the taxpayers of Virginia would wire him the full five million dollars immediately, with three million dollars sent directly to his family to fund his children's education and support his wife through their emotional trauma. The remaining two million dollars would serve as his personal maintenance fund while locked inside his prison cell, and he graciously promised to repay every single cent to the government once he regained his freedom.

The Final Gavel on History's Most Ludicrous Lawsuit

The bizarre handwritten petition landed on the desk of United States District Judge Rebecca Beach Smith, who suddenly found herself evaluating a case unlike anything taught in law school. When reviewing the formal merits of the claim, the court was faced with an inmate who was quite literally asking the justice system to force the government to fund his own personal lawsuit against his own identity. Judge Smith delivered her final decision with razor-sharp judicial clarity and a subtle hint of dry humor. In her official ruling, she noted that Brock had presented an undeniably innovative approach to civil rights litigation, but she dismissed the complaint outright because it lacked any shred of legal validity. She explicitly described the underlying legal premise and the astronomical financial demands as totally ludicrous. It turned out that Brock was already notorious among local court clerks for flooding the judicial system with dozens of frivolous lawsuits, leading the court to ultimately sanction him and restrict his filing privileges to prevent further waste of public resources. Today, law students and legal historians across America and Europe still study the case as a textbook example of courtroom absurdities. In the end, Robert Lee Brock made history by filing a legendary lawsuit against himself, demanding millions from the state, and managing to lose completely to his own reflection.
