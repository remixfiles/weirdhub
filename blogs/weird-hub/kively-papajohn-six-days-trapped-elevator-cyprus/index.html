Six Days In A Dead Elevator: The 76-Year-Old Woman Who Survived What Should Have Been Impossible

By Eram Hanaf • 8 October, 2026

The Night the Elevator Became a Prison

On December 28, 1987, 76-year-old Kively Papajohn went shopping in Limassol, Cyprus, expecting to return home like she had done countless times before. She was carrying a shopping bag filled with groceries, including fruit, vegetables and bread. Kively had spent more than four decades living in Manhattan, New York, where she used elevators several times a day without ever experiencing a serious problem. After returning to her birthplace of Limassol, however, an ordinary trip home suddenly became a terrifying fight for survival. She entered the elevator of the four-story apartment building on Anexartisias Street and headed toward her apartment on the fourth floor. The elevator stopped at the second floor and would not move again. At first, Kively tried to remain calm. She shouted for help and repeatedly banged on the elevator walls and doors, hoping someone outside would hear her. But there was a terrible reason why nobody answered. Although the building stood in a busy shopping area, Kively was the only person living there at the time. The other apartments were empty. Her cries disappeared into the walls of the building, while people continued their normal lives outside without knowing that an elderly woman was trapped only a short distance away. The elevator itself was extremely small, making the situation even more difficult. According to Kively's later account, she could not even lie down inside it. She could not sleep properly during the night and spent hours waiting for daylight so she could begin calling and banging for help again. She later recalled feeling frightened, cold and increasingly desperate. She had no supply of drinking water with her, and she quickly understood that getting out would not be easy. The food in her shopping bag became her only possible source of survival. But even then, she did not simply eat everything. She tried to make the small amount of food last as long as possible. In fact, she initially avoided eating because she was worried about needing to use the toilet while trapped inside the elevator. Eventually, however, she ate some of the tomatoes she had bought because she needed to maintain enough strength to continue fighting for her life. Outside the elevator, nobody yet knew what had happened to her. Her disappearance did not immediately seem unusual because Kively had told relatives that she planned to spend several days in Paphos. That simple misunderstanding would keep the elevator silent for days while her condition became more dangerous with every passing hour.

Six Days Against Hunger, Thirst and Silence

The second day passed with no rescue. Kively continued trying to attract attention, but the building remained silent. There was no neighbor coming down the hallway, no familiar voice calling her name and no maintenance worker checking the elevator. She was completely alone. On the third day, the physical effects of dehydration, lack of food and exhaustion became much more serious. Kively later recalled that she became so weak that she could no longer remain standing. She crouched down to rest her legs, but when she tried to get up again, she discovered that she could not. From that point onward, she remained in that position for the next several days. The elevator had become more than a place where she was trapped; it had become the only environment in which she had to survive. By the fourth day, her body had become so weak that she no longer felt hunger or thirst in the same way. She continued eating small amounts of the food she had brought with her, carefully rationing what remained. The arrival of January 1, 1988, made the situation even more striking. While people around the world were celebrating the beginning of a new year, Kively had already spent four days inside a dark, confined elevator. She had no idea when, or even whether, anyone would find her. Her memories of the fifth and sixth days were incomplete. She later described those days as a kind of trance. Her body was running out of energy, and her ability to think clearly was fading. Yet outside the elevator, the search for her had finally begun to take shape. One of her nieces eventually tried to contact her in Paphos and discovered that Kively had never arrived there. That was the moment the family realized that something was seriously wrong. Authorities were contacted, and a rescue team went to her apartment building. The rescuers entered the building and made their way toward the apartment using the stairs. They began breaking down the apartment door because Kively was not inside. The noise was crucial. After days of near silence, the sound finally reached the trapped woman. Somehow, despite her extreme weakness, Kively gathered what little strength remained and started shouting again. This time, someone heard her. The rescuers discovered that the missing 76-year-old woman was not somewhere far away in another city, as her relatives had initially believed. She was inside the elevator of her own apartment building. She had been there for six days. By then, she had survived without drinking water by eating and rationing the groceries in her shopping bag. Her ordeal had pushed her body close to its limits, but the rescue team arrived before it was too late.

The Rescue That Became a World Record

On January 2, 1988, after six days trapped inside the elevator, Kively Papajohn was finally rescued alive. She was immediately taken to hospital, where doctors treated her for dehydration and the effects of the ordeal. She had also suffered psychological shock and abrasions on her hands and feet from repeatedly hitting the elevator walls while trying to attract attention. Kively later described just how desperately she had fought to be heard. She said that she had banged on the walls so hard that a stone from her ring came loose and fell somewhere inside the elevator shaft. Doctors believed that the timing of the rescue was extremely important. According to the Guinness World Records account, they said Kively would probably not have survived another day without being found. Her survival was remarkable not simply because she was 76 years old, but because she had endured six days in a confined elevator without a normal supply of drinking water or outside assistance. The food she happened to buy that morning became an unexpected lifeline. Guinness World Records officially lists Kively Papajohn as holding the record for the longest time trapped in a lift, with the incident lasting from December 28, 1987, to January 2, 1988. The Guinness record notes that she survived the cold and dehydration by rationing fruit, vegetables and bread from her shopping bag. Guinness later reported that her six-day ordeal was more than twice the previous record of 62 hours, which had been set by Englishman Graham Coates in 1986. The story also received renewed attention decades later. In 2024, Guinness World Records published a detailed account of Kively's experience, bringing the extraordinary survival story back to public attention. Cypriot media have also revisited the case, including reports referring to an original January 1988 newspaper article from Haravgi and archival material from Cyprus's Press and Information Office. After recovering, Kively reportedly abandoned her plan to retire permanently in Cyprus and returned to New York, where she lived with her sons for another eight years. She died in 1996. Her story remains unusual because the record was never something she wanted to achieve; it was created by a terrifying accident that placed her life in danger. What saved her was not a rescue plan, modern technology or a large emergency supply. It was persistence, a small bag of groceries, the decision to ration food, and finally the sound of rescuers breaking through her apartment door. For six days, an ordinary elevator became a silent prison. On the sixth day, it became the place where an extraordinary survival story ended. Kively Papajohn entered that elevator simply trying to go home. She emerged six days later as the woman officially recorded by Guinness World Records for surviving the longest known period trapped inside a lift.
