The Weeping Statue of Mumbai and the Toilet Leak That Forced a Skeptic Into Exile

By WeirdHub Team • 13 November, 2026

A Sudden Phenomenon in Vile Parle

In early March 2012, an ordinary neighborhood in Mumbai became the center of intense religious devotion and widespread media attention. Near the Church of Our Lady of Velankanni in the Vile Parle area of Irla, a roadside crucifix bearing a statue of Jesus began dripping water from its feet. Beginning on March 5, 2012, the steady trickle of droplets quickly drew the notice of local residents and passersby. Within hours, word spread across the bustling Indian metropolis that a divine miracle was unfolding right on the street. Crowds gathered around the shrine in large numbers, eager to witness the unexplained event with their own eyes. Many onlookers viewed the dripping water as a sacred blessing, believing it possessed holy properties capable of bringing healing and good fortune. Driven by deep faith, devotees pushed forward to collect the droplets in containers, cup their hands beneath the feet of the statue, and drink the water reverently. For three consecutive days, until March 8, 2012, the site remained packed with believers praying, singing hymns, and filming the phenomenon on their phones. Television crews and local reporters arrived to document the swelling crowds, turning a quiet corner of Vile Parle into a national spectacle. In a city where faith and daily life are deeply intertwined, the sight of water emerging from the feet of a religious statue without an obvious rainfall or visible hose seemed extraordinary to those standing in the street. The emotional atmosphere was charged with awe, as people sought comfort and spiritual reassurance from what they earnestly believed was a supernatural sign. Yet, while thousands celebrated the occurrence as an act of divine intervention, the sudden appearance of water on a dry day also raised practical questions among observers who wondered if a much simpler physical explanation was hidden behind the wall.

Tracing the Dripping Water to Its True Source

To examine the phenomenon objectively, Mumbai television channel TV9 invited Sanal Edamaruku, a prominent Indian rationalist and investigator of supernatural claims, to visit the site and inspect the statue. Accompanied by cameras, Edamaruku approached the crucifix not with hostility, but with the methodical curiosity of a seasoned investigator looking for basic physical causes. Rather than focusing solely on the droplets falling from the toes of the figure, he examined the surrounding structure, the base of the shrine, and the wall positioned directly behind the statue. Through careful observation, he noticed that moisture was seeping through the masonry and traveling upward and outward along the surface. He explained that the movement of the liquid was driven by capillary action, a well-known physical process in which water flows through narrow spaces within porous materials like brick and concrete without the assistance of external forces. Following the trail of dampness to its origin, Edamaruku traced the moisture behind the wall to an overflowing drainage system connected to a nearby toilet pipe. Faulty plumbing had caused wastewater from the toilet drain to back up and seep continuously into the adjacent wall, saturating the structure until it found an exit point along the exterior of the crucifix and dripped down from the feet of the statue. What thousands of people had been collecting and drinking as holy water was, in reality, contaminated drainage runoff from a leaking toilet pipe. Edamaruku demonstrated his findings clearly on live television, pointing out the plumbing flaw and showing how basic infrastructure failure had created the illusion of a weeping statue. The broadcast immediately stripped away the mystery, replacing supernatural speculation with an undeniable, grounded, and deeply unhygienic reality.

The Heavy Price of Exposing an Uncomfortable Truth

Although Sanal Edamaruku had relied on simple observation and basic physics to protect public health and clarify a misunderstanding, the aftermath of his investigation proved severe. Instead of welcoming the revelation that people were inadvertently drinking contaminated water, several religious groups reacted with outrage, accusing the rationalist of mocking their faith and hurting their religious sentiments. Within weeks of the television broadcast, multiple criminal complaints were filed against Edamaruku across Mumbai under Section 295A of the Indian Penal Code, a law that criminalizes deliberate and malicious acts intended to outrage religious feelings. The legal pressure escalated rapidly, turning a straightforward plumbing inspection into a high-stakes battle over free expression and scientific inquiry. Facing the real prospect of arrest, prolonged detention without bail, and threats to his personal safety, Edamaruku made the difficult decision to leave India. In July 2012, just four months after inspecting the dripping statue in Vile Parle, he traveled to Finland, where he remained in exile to avoid prosecution. His departure sparked international debate among human rights advocates, scientists, and journalists, many of whom highlighted the stark contrast between his benign, evidence-based investigation and the harsh legal consequences he faced. Reports from major global outlets, including The Guardian, the BBC, New Scientist, and fact-checking organizations like Snopes, documented every stage of the saga, preserving the verified facts for audiences around the world. Ultimately, the 2012 Mumbai incident became a defining case study in how easily ordinary infrastructure failures can be mistaken for miracles, and how challenging it can be to present empirical evidence when deeply held beliefs are involved.
