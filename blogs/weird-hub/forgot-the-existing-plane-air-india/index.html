The Phantom Jetliner: How an Airline Completely Forgot It Owned a Boeing 737 for 12 Years

By Helium Observer • 10 September, 2026

A Ghost Plane on the Tarmac

Imagine leaving a massive commercial aircraft parked in a corner of an international airport and simply walking away, forgetting it ever existed. That is precisely what unfolded at Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose International Airport in Kolkata. Back in 2012, a Boeing 737-200 cargo jet originally flown under Alliance Air touched down and was directed to an isolated parking bay due to technical issues. As weeks turned into months, the aircraft quietly disappeared from operational rosters. Bureaucratic chaos, shifting management, and messy paper trails swallowed the identity of the plane. Year after year, the tropical sun baked the twin-engine jet, and seasonal monsoon rains coated its aluminum skin in dust and grime. Yet, while airport ground staff drove past the sleeping giant every day, headquarters acted as if the multi-ton machine had vanished into thin air.

The Bill Nobody Wanted to Pay

The airport authority, however, never suffered from amnesia. Tracking every movement on their tarmac, officials sent recurring invoices to Air India for parking fees, racking up charges year after year. The response from the airline was an absolute denial. Company representatives repeatedly rejected the demands, adamantly insisting that the plane was not theirs, had never belonged to their fleet, and was essentially someone else’s problem. This bureaucratic stalemate dragged on for more than a decade until airport managers finally ran out of patience. They urgently needed the valuable real estate to build modern hangars and taxiways to support growing international traffic. Under serious pressure to clear the apron, the carrier, now under the fresh management of the Tata Group, conducted an exhaustive asset audit. When analysts dug through dusty archives, the truth struck like lightning: the phantom aircraft really was their own.

A Strange Rescue and a Second Life

The revelation turned into one of modern aviation history’s most bizarre chapters. Air India CEO Campbell Wilson officially acknowledged the discovery in an internal memo, dryly noting how unusual it was to stumble upon an entire airliner that the company owned without knowing it. To settle the embarrassing oversight, the airline cleared the accumulated parking tab, which had climbed to roughly one hundred and fifteen thousand dollars. Because the old Boeing was no longer flight-worthy after twelve years of idle abandonment, technicians carefully dismantled its wings and loaded the heavy fuselage onto specialized flatbed trucks. The convoy hauled the aircraft across thousands of miles to Bengaluru, where it was transformed into a hands-on training rig for young aviation engineers. Interestingly, this jet was not entirely alone in its prolonged solitude, as authorities revealed they had cleared fourteen forgotten airframes from Kolkata’s backlots over five years, turning a decade of bureaucratic confusion into a fascinating legend of the skies.
