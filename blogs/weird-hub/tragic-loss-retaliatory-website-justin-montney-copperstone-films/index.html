A Tragic Loss and a Refused Refund

By WeirdHub Team • 6 November, 2026

In early 2020, Justin Montney and his twenty-two-year-old fiancée, Alexis Watts, were eagerly preparing for what was meant to be one of the happiest days of their lives. The couple had formally set their wedding date for May 23, 2020, and were actively organizing the various vendor arrangements required for their upcoming ceremony in Colorado. As part of these essential preparations, they hired a local videography company named Copperstone Films to film and record the memorable moments of their special day. To officially reserve the videography team for the scheduled date, Justin paid an advance deposit of 1,800 US dollars. However, their joyous anticipation came to a devastating and sudden end in February 2020 when an unexpected tragedy occurred. Alexis was involved in a fatal automobile crash on Interstate 70 in Colorado and tragically passed away. The sudden loss left Justin utterly heartbroken and grieving, faced with the overwhelming pain of losing his life partner just three months before their planned wedding. In the midst of this profound personal grief, Justin was forced to undertake the agonizing task of reaching out to vendors to cancel all existing wedding arrangements. When he contacted Copperstone Films to inform them of the fatal accident and request a refund of the 1,800 dollar advance deposit, the videography company firmly refused to return the funds. The company maintained that, according to the official contract terms signed during the booking process, all advance payments were non-refundable regardless of any unforeseen circumstances. Justin openly acknowledged that the written agreement contained a non-refundable clause, but he respectfully pleaded with the firm to reconsider its policy in light of the extraordinary tragedy. He hoped the company would show human empathy given the untimely death of his twenty-two-year-old fiancée. Despite his respectful request for compassion, Copperstone Films refused to alter its position, holding tightly to the advance payment and refusing to offer any compromise or refund during an extraordinarily painful period for the grieving groom.

Media Attention and Escalating Conflict

Disappointed by the unyielding stance of Copperstone Films and feeling that the company showed a complete lack of basic empathy, Justin Montney decided to share his frustrating experience with the local media. He brought the story to the attention of local news station Fox31, also known as KDVR in Denver, Colorado, in the hope that media attention might prompt the business owners to reevaluate their rigid decision. When Fox31 broadcasted the report detailing how the videography company refused to refund a deposit following a bride's fatal accident, the story quickly gained public attention across the state and beyond. Severe public backlash and sharp criticism began to mount against Copperstone Films as viewers expressed outrage over the firm's uncompassionate behavior. However, instead of offering an apology, attempting to resolve the matter privately, or quietly upholding their contract stance, the videography company reacted with unprecedented hostility. Copperstone Films went so far as to launch a targeted website registered under Justin's actual name, using the domain address JustinMontney.com. On this custom website, the company publicly uploaded private email conversations and message exchanges that had taken place between Justin and their staff members. To make matters even more contentious, Copperstone Films boldly posted a statement claiming that they intended to use the withheld 1,800 dollar deposit from Justin to keep the retaliatory domain live and hosted on the internet for two full years. This extraordinary reaction turned a standard contractual dispute over advance money into a public cyberbullying incident. The creation of the retaliatory site shocked the local community and sparked intense online discussions regarding business ethics, customer relations, and the total breakdown of respect toward a grieving individual.

The Cruel Wedding Day Harassment and Global Backlash

The disturbing conflict reached a painful climax on May 23, 2020, which was the exact calendar date on which Justin Montney and Alexis Watts had originally been scheduled to exchange their wedding vows. On that emotionally heartbreaking day, Copperstone Films chose to publish a picture of the couple on the JustinMontney.com website along with exceptionally insensitive remarks targeted directly at Justin. The videography company wrote a message stating that they hoped Justin would spend the entire day crying his eyes out on the date that was supposed to be his wedding day. Furthermore, the company added a blunt and callous note expressing that while they might say sorry, they felt no actual remorse or regret, using the harsh wording "Sorry, but not sorry." The deliberate timing of this post on what should have been the couple's wedding day ignited a massive international wave of disgust and public indignation. Major national and international media outlets, including NBC News, The Denver Post, and Insider, covered the story, bringing widespread attention to the cruel statements made by the company. Thousands of people around the world voiced their outrage over the company's lack of basic decency and human respect toward a young man who had lost his fiancée only months earlier in a fatal crash on Interstate 70. What had started as a simple request to return an 1,800 dollar advance payment transformed into a globally recognized news story about internet harassment and corporate ethics. Ultimately, Justin was forced to endure severe public cyberbullying on top of his unimaginable grief, leaving a lasting impression on audiences worldwide who followed the tragic and contentious sequence of events.
