Frozen Solid for Eighty Minutes: The Swedish Doctor Who Came Back from the Dead

By WH Team • 25 September, 2026

Trapped Beneath the Roaring Torrent

On a crisp afternoon in May 1999, Swedish radiologist in training Anna Bagenholm was skiing down a familiar mountainside near Narvik in northern Norway. What was meant to be a relaxed outing with two fellow doctors turned into a living nightmare within seconds. As she negotiated a steep gully along an alpine stream, Anna suddenly lost control on the hard snow, slipped, and fell headfirst onto an icy crust covering the frozen waterfall. The ice immediately gave way beneath her weight. She slid into the roaring, freezing water underneath a slab of ice eight inches thick, wedged tightly upside down between rocks and the frozen sheet above. Only her ski boots and lower legs remained visible to her horrified companions. Desperately digging with their bare hands and ski poles, her friends tried to pull her out, but the vicious current held her body in a crushing vice. Miraculously, beneath the darkness and churning ice water, Anna found a tiny air pocket between the water surface and the ice ceiling. For forty terrifying minutes, she fought for survival in complete freezing darkness, breathing shallow sips of trapped air while her body temperature plunged at a lethal rate. Eventually, exhausted, suffocated, and paralyzed by the brutal arctic cold, her struggles ceased, her heart stopped beating entirely, and silence took over. By the time emergency rescuers finally cut through the ice and pulled her out, eighty agonizing minutes had passed. Anna had no pulse, no respiration, her pupils were wide and unreactive, and her skin was ghost white and ice-cold to the touch. By every known clinical metric, she was dead.

The Nine-Hour Battle Against Clinical Death

The mountain rescue team immediately began continuous cardiopulmonary resuscitation on the snow, refusing to give up as a rescue helicopter raced against twilight toward Tromso University Hospital. When Anna arrived at the emergency bay, more than three hours had passed since her heart took its last beat. Her core body temperature was measured at an unbelievable 13.7 degrees Celsius, or 56.7 degrees Fahrenheit, the lowest body temperature ever recorded in a human being with eventual survival. An electrocardiogram showed a flatline, confirming complete circulatory arrest. To the casual observer, treating a frozen corpse seemed completely futile, but emergency physician Dr. Mads Gilbert and his surgical colleagues adhered strictly to an ancient medical adage: nobody is dead until they are warm and dead. More than a hundred doctors, nurses, and perfusionists mobilized in a coordinated medical offensive. Because ordinary warming blankets would cause fatal tissue breakdown, the surgical team quickly hooked Anna up to a cardiopulmonary bypass machine, diverting her thick, frigid blood outside her body, gently heating and oxygenating it, and pumping it back through her veins. Slowly, degree by degree, her inner organs were brought back from near absolute zero. For nearly nine relentless hours, the hospital team labored over her frozen chest. Then, at around four in the afternoon, her heart stirred with a faint, irregular rhythm, gathered strength, and began to pump blood entirely on its own. Anna had officially crossed the boundary back from clinical death.



A Painful Awakening and an Unbelievable Triumph

Reviving Anna’s heart was only the first chapter of an excruciatingly difficult journey. She remained in a deep coma on a mechanical ventilator in the intensive care unit for thirty-five days, her organs suspended in fragile equilibrium. When she finally opened her eyes and regained full consciousness, the relief of her loved ones was met by a crushing reality. Severe damage to her peripheral nervous system left her completely paralyzed from the neck down, unable to move a single finger. In those early, agonizing months of bedridden torment, Anna was overwhelmed by intense nerve pain and even felt deep anger toward her colleagues for saving her into a broken body. Yet beneath the pain lay an unbreakable will. Week by week, through brutal physical therapy, her nerves began the slow, microscopic process of regeneration. Her kidneys and digestive system resumed normal function, her limbs regained strength, and sensation gradually returned to her hands and feet. Within a year, she was walking, laughing, and defying every bleak prediction medical science could offer. Although slight nerve numbness lingered in her hands, Anna eventually returned to the medical field, working as a fully qualified senior radiologist at the very same hospital in Tromso that had saved her life. Her survival rewrote emergency medicine textbooks worldwide, proving that extreme cold can freeze human cells into a protective state of suspended animation, preserving brain function against total starvation of oxygen. Today, her miraculous resurrection stands as an enduring testament to human endurance, scientific courage, and the stubborn refusal to surrender hope in the face of impossible darkness.
