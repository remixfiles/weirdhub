The Green-Faced Thief Who Couldn't Hide From Anyone

By Daniel Mathew • 9 September, 2026

A Disguise Gone Horribly Wrong

In May 2018, in the Russian city of Krasnodar, a 23-year-old man came up with what he thought was a clever plan. He wanted to steal a woman's purse at the local train station, but he was worried that witnesses might later identify him to the police. So he decided to cover his entire face in bright green paint, hoping it would make him unrecognizable. Confident in his disguise, he approached the woman at the station, snatched her purse, and disappeared into the crowd, believing he had gotten away with the perfect crime.

The Plan Backfires Instantly

The woman quickly reported the theft to the police, and officers rushed to the train station to speak with witnesses. What they heard next made the case almost laughably easy. Every single witness described the same thing: a young man with a completely green face. In a city of roughly 750,000 people, a green-faced man was not blending into the crowd, he was standing out from it. Instead of hiding his identity, the paint had turned him into the most noticeable person in the entire area. Police officers began searching the streets near the station, and it did not take long before they spotted someone matching that unmistakable description. The thief was arrested within a short time, and remarkably, he still had the stolen purse with him, he had not even had the chance to get rid of the evidence before he was caught.

The Truth Behind the Green Paint

As the story spread, people around the world wanted to know exactly what substance the man had used. It turned out to be zelyonka, a bright green antiseptic liquid that is extremely common in Russian households, often found in medicine cabinets and used to treat small cuts and scrapes. While it is harmless on skin, zelyonka is notoriously difficult to wash off, sometimes taking almost a week to fully disappear naturally. In Russia, this same dye has even been used in political protests, where it was thrown on the faces of activists and opposition figures as a form of public humiliation. In this case, however, the thief used it entirely on his own, believing it would protect his identity. Instead, it did the exact opposite, guaranteeing that anyone who saw him would remember him instantly. The story was first reported by Oddity Central and later picked up by international outlets, quickly becoming one of those unforgettable real-life examples of a criminal plan defeating itself. In the end, the very thing meant to keep him hidden became the clearest signal pointing straight to him, proving that sometimes the smartest-sounding idea can be the one that gives everything away.
