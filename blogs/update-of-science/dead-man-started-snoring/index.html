DEAD FOR FOUR HOURS: THE SPANISH PRISONER WHO WOKE UP ON THE AUTOPSY TABLE

By Josephine • 13 September, 2026

The Morning Everything Went Wrong

On the morning of January 7, 2018, guards at Villabona Prison near Oviedo, in the Asturias region of northern Spain, noticed something unusual during the daily head count. One inmate, 29 year old Gonzalo Montoya Jimenez, failed to appear. When staff checked his cell, they found him slumped in a chair, completely unresponsive. His family later said he had swallowed pills in an apparent suicide attempt. Two doctors on duty at the prison rushed to examine him. They checked his pulse, his breathing, every sign they knew to look for. They found nothing. No heartbeat, no breath, no response to stimuli. Convinced there was nothing more to be done, they declared him dead. An hour later, a forensic doctor arrived to confirm the findings before the body could be released for autopsy. He performed his own examination, reached the same grim conclusion, and signed a third official death certificate. Three separate medical professionals, using standard procedures, had now agreed on the same verdict. There was no reason left to doubt it. Jimenez's body was placed in a body bag and transported to the forensic institute in Oviedo, ready for the routine post mortem examination that follows any unexplained death in custody.

Marked for the Scalpel

At the mortuary, the process continued exactly as it would for any other case. Jimenez's body was moved into cold storage to preserve it until the pathologists were ready. When the time came, technicians began the standard pre autopsy preparation, which included drawing guideline marks on his skin with a scalpel to indicate where the first incisions would be made. Everything about the scene suggested a routine, almost mechanical procedure, the kind performed countless times in mortuaries around the world. Four hours had now passed since he was first declared dead. Then, just before the pathologists were set to begin, something stopped them cold. A sound was coming from inside the sealed body bag. At first it was faint, easy to dismiss as some trick of the equipment or the room. But it did not stop. It was rhythmic, unmistakable, human. It was snoring. The forensic team froze, then quickly unzipped the bag. Inside, Gonzalo Montoya Jimenez was breathing. He was alive. His father later told reporters that his son later described the terrifying sensation of realizing, as if surfacing from the bottom of a deep well, that people were lifting him out of a cover and placing him on a table, preparing to cut him open.

A Body That Lied to Everyone

Jimenez was rushed by ambulance to the Central University Hospital of Asturias, where he was placed in intensive care and gradually regained full consciousness. Doctors began searching for an explanation, and they found one in his family history. Jimenez suffered from epilepsy, and specialists believe he experienced a rare and extreme episode of catalepsy, a condition in which the body enters a trance like state, muscles rigid, breathing and heartbeat slowed to nearly imperceptible levels. In severe cases, catalepsy can mimic death so convincingly that even trained doctors, using standard clinical checks, can be fooled. It is a condition documented in medical literature for centuries, often cited in old stories of people buried alive, though modern medicine had believed such total misdiagnoses were now essentially impossible. This case suggested otherwise. Spain's prison authorities and the regional government of Asturias opened a formal investigation into how three independent medical examinations could all reach the same wrong conclusion, and the story quickly spread far beyond Spain, picked up by international outlets including CBS News, ABC News and ScienceAlert. For Jimenez, it became a case study, a name attached to one of the strangest medical near misses on record. For everyone else who read about it, it left an uneasy question hanging in the air, one that medicine still has not fully answered: how do you know, with absolute certainty, when someone is truly gone.
