The Dog Who Ratted Out His Own Owner to the Police

By Oppa Meen • 10 September, 2026

A Wild Chase on Two Wheels

It started as a normal February evening in Runcorn, England, until it turned into something straight out of a comedy movie. Charles Foy, 41, was riding an illegal e-scooter down a public road with his two dogs trotting beside him. Riding an e-scooter on public roads is against the law in the UK because these vehicles cannot legally be insured. When a police officer spotted him and signaled him to stop, the officer also noticed a strong smell of cannabis coming from Foy's direction. Instead of pulling over, Foy panicked and sped off, trying to outrun the police on his little electric scooter. It might have seemed like a smart escape plan at first, but there was one huge problem. Foy had brought his two loyal dogs along for the ride, and one of them, a Labrador, simply could not keep up with the scooter's speed. As Foy vanished into the distance, his own dog was left behind, confused and alone on the street, unaware that it was about to become the star witness in its owner's arrest.

A Loyal Dog Leads the Way Home

This is where the story takes its most unexpected turn. Instead of giving up, the police officer got out of his car and decided to follow the abandoned Labrador, hoping it might lead somewhere useful. And it did, in the most direct way possible. The dog walked calmly and confidently straight toward its own home, with the officer following close behind the entire time. When they reached the house, the officer even asked the dog on camera, in a lighthearted tone, whether its owner had been a bad boy and left it behind. Through a ground floor window, officers then spotted the second dog that had also been out with Foy earlier, which strongly suggested this was indeed the right address. What seemed like a random pet reunion had actually turned into an unintentional police escort, guided entirely by animal instinct and loyalty toward home.

Caught Hiding in the Attic

Once inside the house, officers searched every room and eventually found Charles Foy hiding in the attic, tucked away under the insulation in an attempt to stay hidden from sight. He reportedly claimed he had not been riding the e-scooter that night at all and said he had simply been sleeping upstairs, but the evidence, along with his own dog, told a very different story. The court was not convinced by his explanation either. Foy was sentenced to three months in prison, largely because he had been driving despite an existing ban, used a vehicle that could not be insured, and refused to take a drug test when asked. On top of the prison sentence, he was also banned from driving any vehicle for four years and three months. Cheshire Police only released this story publicly in mid August, months after the actual incident took place, and it quickly caught attention online. In the end, it was not fingerprints, cameras, or detective work that cracked the case, but simple, unconditional canine loyalty leading the police right to the door.
