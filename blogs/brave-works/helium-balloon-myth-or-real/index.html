The Man Who Flew to 16,000 Feet in a Backyard Lawn Chair

By TR Mohona • 8 September, 2026

A Dangerous Dream Born in a Suburban Backyard

On a quiet summer morning on July 2, 1982, in San Pedro, California, a thirty-three-year-old truck driver named Larry Walters decided to make his childhood fantasy come true. Having been rejected by the United States Air Force due to poor eyesight, Larry never let go of his burning ambition to float into the sky. Sitting in his girlfriend’s backyard, he attached forty-two heavy-duty helium weather balloons to an ordinary aluminium garden patio chair that he humorously christened Inspiration I. His inventory was modest yet wild: an altimeter, a CB walkie-talkie, some sandwiches, cold sodas, and a pellet gun. His original plan seemed deceptively straightforward. He intended to cut the ground tethers, float peacefully about thirty to one hundred feet above the grass, sip his drinks for an hour, and then shoot down a few balloons to drift safely back to earth. However, physics and raw lifting power had completely different plans. The moment his friends severed the main cord holding the chair down, the homemade aircraft rocketed upward at an alarming speed of nearly one thousand feet per minute, completely defying his naive calculations.

Frozen Skies and Baffled Airline Pilots

Within minutes, the green suburban lawns shrank into distant patches as Larry broke through the cloud ceiling, climbing straight toward sixteen thousand feet into freezing thin air. Dressed only in casual clothes and sunglasses, he found himself shivering uncontrollably in sub-zero temperatures with no oxygen tank and no protective gear. To make the bizarre situation far more perilous, his helium contraption drifted straight into the primary approach corridor of Los Angeles International Airport, one of the busiest air hubs in the entire world. Airline captains flying commercial jetliners for TWA and Delta suddenly looked out their cockpit windows to witness an unbelievable sight: an ordinary man sitting casually in a backyard patio chair, drifting through active airliner lanes with a gun resting across his lap. Radio communications crackled in total disbelief as air traffic controllers scrambled to figure out whether the radar blip was a flying hoax or a deadly disaster unfolding in real time. Larry stayed suspended between life and death for nearly two freezing hours.

The Desperate Descent and an Unforgettable Legacy

Realizing that hypothermia or asphyxiation would soon take his life, Larry knew he had to act fast despite his frozen fingers. He raised his pellet gun and carefully began shooting out balloons one by one to stop the upward climb and initiate a descent. In the freezing chaos, he accidentally dropped the gun overboard, but luckily, he had popped enough balloons to begin sinking back toward civilization. The terrifying ride finally came to an end when his trailing balloon strings tangled directly into high-voltage power lines in a Long Beach residential neighborhood. The impact instantly triggered a blackout that cut electricity to the entire area for twenty minutes, but miraculously, Larry climbed down totally unharmed. Police officers quickly swarmed the scene to place him in handcuffs, while the Federal Aviation Administration slapped him with major airspace safety fines. When a breathless reporter pushed a microphone into his face and asked why he did it, Larry delivered an immortal line that captured the human spirit: A man cannot just sit around.
