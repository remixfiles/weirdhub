The Baboon Who Went to War: How Corporal Jackie Conquered the Trenches of World War One

By Nadra Miammi • 17 September, 2026

From Farmyard Companion to Decorated Trench Sentinel

In 1915, as the flames of the Great War consumed nations across the globe, an ordinary South African farmer named Albert Marr stepped forward to enlist in the 3rd South African Infantry Regiment. Albert carried an extraordinarily unusual request: he refused to leave behind his clever pet chacma baboon, Jackie, whom he had found injured and adopted on his farm near Pretoria. To the surprise of many, commanding officers agreed to let the baboon accompany the regiment, assuming he would merely serve as an amusing mascot to keep spirits high. However, Jackie proved to be far more than just a novelty pet. He was formally sworn into military service, assigned an official service number, provided with a tailored soldier uniform, a cap, and his own military paybook. Jackie rapidly learned military discipline, sharing mess rations with proper etiquette using a knife and fork, lighting cigarettes for his comrades, and executing crisp, textbook salutes whenever superior officers passed by. His presence went far beyond morale boosting when the regiment shipped out to combat zones in Egypt and across Europe. Baboons possess senses of hearing and night vision vastly superior to humans, and Jackie soon became an invaluable night sentry. In the dead of night, long before human ears could detect approaching German raiding parties, Jackie would give a low warning growl or tug at Albert's sleeve, saving dozens of fellow soldiers from surprise assaults and establishing himself as a reliable brother-in-arms.

Bravery Under Fire and the Cost of Survival

The true test of courage arrived when the South African forces were thrust into the grim, mud-soaked trenches of the Western Front in France and Belgium. During the brutal fighting at the Battle of Agagia in Egypt earlier in the campaign, Albert had taken a bullet to the shoulder; Jackie stayed faithfully by his side, gently licking the wound and keeping him conscious until field medics arrived. By 1918, during violent shell bombardments in Belgium, the horrors of modern industrial warfare struck directly at their company. As artillery shells pulverized their trench line, Jackie desperately attempted to stack heavy stones around himself and Albert, trying instinctively to build a protective wall against the lethal rain of shrapnel. A high-explosive shell detonated nearby, sending jagged steel fragments tearing through the makeshift shelter. Jackie was gravely wounded in his right leg and arm, while Albert suffered severe blast trauma. Field doctors immediately rushed the wounded baboon to the casualty clearing station, where the battalion medical officer performed an emergency operation under chloroform, forced to amputate the baboon's shattered leg. For his extraordinary valor, loyalty, and sacrifices in the line of duty, Jackie was officially promoted to the rank of corporal and awarded the prestigious Pretoria Citizens Service Medal, earning the eternal respect of every man who witnessed his unflinching bravery.

A Hero's Welcome and an Enduring Legend

Following months of recuperation in military hospitals in England, where his recovery made headlines across Allied newspapers, Corporal Jackie and Albert returned to South Africa at the conclusion of the war. Jackie was given a hero's reception during victory parades, dressed proudly in his decorated service jacket with three wound chevrons and a blue service stripe. In Cape Town and Johannesburg, Jackie attended massive public charity events to raise funds for disabled soldiers, war widows, and orphans, allowing citizens to shake his hand or donate money to kiss the legendary veteran soldier. At the Maitland Dispersal Camp in April 1919, Jackie was formally discharged from the armed forces with complete military honors, official discharge papers, and a military pension for his honorable wartime service. Together, Albert and his beloved corporal returned to their peaceful family farm to live out their days away from the nightmare of trench warfare. Tragedy struck in 1921 when a devastating fire swept through the farmstead, claiming Jackie's life, followed decades later by Albert, who passed away in 1973 after forever preserving the memory of his bravest companion. Today, Corporal Jackie remains one of the most remarkable military legends in World War One history, a moving symbol of cross-species loyalty and courage under the darkest clouds of war.
